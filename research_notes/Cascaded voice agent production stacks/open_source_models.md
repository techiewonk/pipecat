# Open-source / open-weight models per layer of a cascaded voice agent (STT → LLM → TTS), as of November 2026

Research date: 2026-11-17. Reading notes for the report writer:

- "Vendor figure" = number published by the model's maker, not independently reproduced. Most TTS first-audio numbers and all concurrency numbers below are vendor figures.
- Many comparison tables come from secondary blogs (Micdrop, Dograh, Pinggy, Cartesia, gpuwerk, quantacloud, vdf.ai). These are vendors or aggregators that say they read licenses and model cards directly; I cite them as such. Primary sources (model cards, repos, license files, papers, Pipecat/LiveKit/Kyutai/NVIDIA/Modal/Daily/Baseten pages) are used wherever I reached them.
- Open ASR Leaderboard WERs are **not comparable across dates**: the English average changed from 8 public sets to 11 sets (two private) during 2026, and different articles quote different snapshots. Conflicts are flagged inline.

## 1. Streaming STT/ASR: which open models are production-viable for real time?

### Takeaway
By November 2026 the open streaming-ASR default for English voice agents is NVIDIA's cache-aware Nemotron Speech Streaming 0.6B (221 ms median time-to-final-transcript on Pipecat's benchmark, faster than every hosted API measured), with Voxtral Mini 4B Realtime (Apache 2.0), Kyutai STT (CC-BY 4.0, built-in semantic VAD), and Moonshine v2 (MIT, CPU) as the other true-streaming options. The most accurate open models (Qwen3-ASR 1.7B, Granite Speech 5.0, Cohere Transcribe, Canary-Qwen, Parakeet TDT) are segment/offline models that work well behind a VAD + turn detector; Whisper is now the ecosystem pick, not the accuracy or latency pick. Hindi is the weak spot: only Nemotron 3.5 (hi-IN "transcription-ready"), Qwen3-ASR, Whisper and AI4Bharat IndicConformer cover it in open weights.

- Hosted reference for Indian languages: Sarvam Saaras V4 (closed API) claims SOTA on all 22 Indian languages and streaming TTFT under 150 ms; I found no open Sarvam speech weights — [Sarvam blog](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/introducing-saaras-v4)

### Inferences
- Two viable architectures: (a) true streaming ASR (Nemotron, Voxtral Realtime, Kyutai) which gives partials and ~20–250 ms finalization; (b) fast offline ASR (Parakeet TDT, Qwen3-ASR, Granite TurboCTC) run once per VAD/turn-detected segment. With RTFx in the thousands, (b) adds only tens of ms and gives better WER, at the cost of no partial transcripts.
- For Hindi/Indic self-hosting, the candidates are Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming (streaming, hi-IN), Qwen3-ASR (Apache 2.0), and IndicConformer (MIT, 22 languages); Whisper's Hindi accuracy in NVIDIA's table (28% WER) argues against it.
- Licensing splits three ways: Apache/MIT (Qwen3-ASR, Voxtral, Granite, Cohere, Moonshine, Whisper), attribution-only CC-BY 4.0 (Parakeet TDT, Canary-Qwen, Kyutai), and NVIDIA-specific terms (NVIDIA Open Model License; OpenMDW-1.1) that permit commercial use but need legal review.

### Gaps
- No independent concurrency-per-GPU numbers for Nemotron streaming, Voxtral Realtime or Qwen3-ASR; all are vendor claims (and Kyutai's 400-stream claim was only partially reproduced by one user).
- sherpa-onnx and Vosk: not researched beyond mentions; license/WER not verified here.
- "Indic-Transcribe" (AI4Bharat successor) details, license and WER not retrieved.
- Open ASR Leaderboard snapshot conflict (Oct 2026 vs Micdrop's Nov 2026 values) not resolved against the live board.
- No Hinglish/code-mix WER for any open model found.

## 2. VAD, turn detection / end-of-turn, and noise suppression

### Takeaway
Silero VAD (MIT) + an audio-native semantic end-of-turn model is the standard open front end; Pipecat Smart Turn v3.2 (BSD-2, 8 MB, 23 languages incl. Hindi) is the only fully open (weights + data + training code) turn model, while LiveKit's newer audio turn detector scores better on LiveKit's own benchmark but ships under a proprietary "LiveKit Model License". Open noise suppression (RNNoise, DeepFilterNet) is free and low-latency but can hurt STT accuracy; the STT-tuned suppressors (Krisp VIVA, ai-coustics Quail) are commercial.

### Inferences
- Self-hosted TTFT of roughly 100–250 ms is achievable for 27–31B models at low concurrency on a single high-end GPU (RTX 5090 / H100 / B200) with FP8/NVFP4/Q4 quantization and warm prefix cache; hosted per-token endpoints of the same models show ~500–650 ms because of network and shared queues. TTFT rises steeply with concurrency (seconds at 32–50 concurrent on one GPU), so voice deployments must cap streams per GPU well below throughput-optimal batch sizes.
- Groq/Cerebras are a latency midpoint, but the model that is fastest there (gpt-oss-120b) is the weakest on tool-calling reliability; Cerebras Gemma 4 31B / Qwen3.8 27B trade ~150 ms more TTFT for much better accuracy.
- Dense ~30B models clearly beat small-active-parameter MoE variants for tool calling in voice.

### Gaps
- No voice-benchmark numbers for Llama 4.x or Mistral open models.
- No controlled TTFT-vs-concurrency data for a 27–31B model with a realistic voice prompt (long system prompt + tools, distinct conversations) on vLLM vs SGLang vs TensorRT-LLM.
- Modal's LLM Almanac values were not retrieved directly.
- Licenses for GLM-5.2, DeepSeek V4 and Kimi K2.6 are described only as "open weights" by the source; exact license names not verified.

## 5. Open speech-to-speech models as an alternative to cascading

### Takeaway
Open full-duplex models (Moshi, PersonaPlex 7B, NemotronLabs VoiceChat 11B) have largely solved turn-taking (~200–450 ms, 91% conversational-dynamics score) but reason far below hosted S2S APIs (4–27% vs 76–99% on Big Bench Audio), mostly lack tool calling and non-English support, and ship no telephony path — so cascades remain the production architecture for self-hosted agents.

## 6. Combinations actually running in production / credible reference implementations, and reported voice-to-voice latency

### Takeaway
Credible fully-open cascades report ~0.5 s voice-to-voice on a single co-located high-end GPU and ~0.9–1.0 s median at production concurrency (NVIDIA: sub-second up to 64 streams on 4x B200; Baseten: 0.94–1.14 s across 1–56 calls; Modal: ~1 s median). - Add ~250 ms (WebRTC) to 300–600 ms (PSTN) to any server-side figure for user-perceived latency.
