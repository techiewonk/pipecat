Conventions used throughout:
- All prices are USD list prices unless marked INR (₹). Where a rupee conversion is shown it uses ~₹92/USD, the rate implied by Bolna's own page ($0.06 = ₹5.52).
- "Checked" dates are the dates the cited source says it read the vendor page; when the vendor page itself is cited, the read date is 17 Nov 2026 (search-engine excerpt, not a logged-in quote).
- Source-quality tags: **[V]** vendor's own page/claim, **[I]** independent measurement, **[A]** third-party aggregator/blog (often a competitor or SEO page; treat as secondary), **[P]** practitioner first-person account, **[R]** peer-reviewed research.

## 1. Cost: hosted platforms, component APIs, self-hosted GPUs, telephony, break-even and hidden costs

### Takeaway
In Nov 2026 hosted orchestration platforms land at roughly $0.07-$0.15/min all-in before telephony (platform fee alone $0.05-$0.08), an open-source framework with API models lands at roughly $0.03-$0.06/min, and fully self-hosted models on GPUs can reach ~$0.01-$0.03/min only at sustained volume and high utilization. Hardware-only break-even is ~7k-15k min/month per GPU, but once engineering/on-call is counted published estimates move it to ~20k-180k min/month, so the platform fee is the cheap part until volume is material.

### Inferences
- Three distinct tiers get conflated as "self-hosted"; they have different break-evens (my arithmetic from the list prices above, USD, US inbound, 800 chars/min TTS, ~2k-token prompt):
  - **Tier 1 - hosted orchestrator** (Vapi/Retell/ElevenLabs/Bland): ~$0.08-$0.15/min + telephony.
  - **Tier 2 - open-source framework + API models** (Pipecat/LiveKit, self-run or their clouds): hosting $0.00-0.01 + STT $0.0025-0.0077 + LLM $0.002-0.005 + TTS $0.012-0.036 + SIP $0.003-0.005 ≈ **$0.02-$0.06/min**. TTS is the dominant line; choosing a $15/1M-char TTS instead of a $40 one halves the bill.
  - **Tier 3 - open models on own GPUs**: ~$0.01-$0.03/min at ≥100k min/mo with good utilization; worse than Tier 2 below ~10k min/mo.
- The Tier 1 → Tier 2 saving is roughly $0.04-$0.08/min. If moving costs 0.5-1 fully loaded engineer (say $8k-$15k/month - assumption), break-even is ~100k-375k min/month; with ~15 h/month of maintenance ($1-2k) it is ~15k-50k min/month. This reconciles the wide spread in published break-evens (20k vs 90k-183k): they differ almost entirely in the engineering headcount assumed.
- Tier 2 → Tier 3 saves another ~$0.02-$0.03/min, mostly TTS. Against cheap TTS/STT APIs ($15/1M chars, $0.15/hr) the cost case for GPUs is weak until several hundred thousand minutes/month; the case is then control, residency, latency and custom voices rather than price.
- Idle capacity is the decisive variable for Tier 3: business-hours traffic uses a 24/7 GPU at well under 33% utilization, which the cited models show multiplies per-minute cost 3-4x.
- Promotional pricing is widespread in Nov 2026 (Deepgram STT promos and Flux TTS credit match to 31 Dec 2026; Gemini Flash doubling 1 Jan 2027; OpenAI tts-1 shutdown 6 Jan 2027), so any Tier 2 cost model built today will shift in Q1 2027.

## 2. Latency: perceived-natural targets, budget breakdown, measured vs claimed, techniques

### Takeaway
Human turn gaps average ~200 ms, the industry working target is ~800 ms voice-to-voice (felt as "broken" near 1.5 s), and independent caller-side measurements of hosted platforms in 2026 show medians of ~1.3-1.7 s on real phone calls on default-ish settings (0.7-0.85 s for tuned stacks), roughly 2-20x vendor headline claims. The two largest and most tunable budget lines are endpointing delay and LLM time-to-first-token; component model latencies (STT ~50-300 ms, TTS TTFB ~100-300 ms) are comparatively small.

### Cited Findings

#### 2a. What humans perceive as natural
- Across 10 languages the mean response offset is +208 ms, overall median +100 ms, modal offset 0-200 ms; language means fall within ~250 ms of the cross-language mean (Danish slowest +469 ms, Japanese fastest +7 ms) — [R] [Stivers et al. 2009, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0903616106)
- A 24-language reanalysis found mean response latency of 59 ms with 46% of languages showing slight overlap; producing even a single word takes ~600 ms to plan, so humans predict turn ends rather than react — [R] [Springer review, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-025-02749-8)
- Answers preceded by an inbreath have a modal latency of 576 ms vs 100 ms without, i.e. natural human replies range widely — [R] [Torreira et al. 2015, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00284/full)
- In an in-vehicle voice-assistant study (only 6 experts) a 1.5 s delay was rated best, ≤5 s tolerable, and the fastest responses produced the most disagreement - "fastest may not be most desirable" — [R] [Sogemeier et al. 2024, HFES](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10711813241260290)
- The 800 ms figure is "an industry-consensus rule of thumb"; no primary research pins abandonment to 800 ms; above ~1,500 ms feels like a walkie-talkie; an untuned cascade lands at 750-1,400 ms — [A] [Softcery, 22 May 2026](https://softcery.com/lab/voice-agent-latency-budget-microphone-to-speaker)
- Production targets quoted: P50 <1.5 s and P95 <5 s for cascaded pipelines, <800 ms aspirational (Softcery); Twilio's "starting benchmarks" (Nov 2025, older): mouth-to-ear turn gap target 1,115 ms / upper limit 1,400 ms; platform turn gap 885 / 1,100 ms; STT 350/500, LLM TTFT 375/750, TTS TTFB 100/250 — [A] [Softcery guide](https://softcery.com/lab/custom-ai-voice-agents-the-ultimate-guide); [V] [Twilio core latency guide](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/best-practices/guide-core-latency-ai-voice-agents)

### Inferences
- To match hosted platforms as callers actually experience them (median ~1.3-1.7 s on phone calls, 0.7-0.85 s when tuned), a self-hosted cascade needs roughly: endpointing ≤250-300 ms (semantic turn model, not a 500-1,000 ms silence timer), STT finalization ≤100-150 ms, LLM first *answer* token ≤350-500 ms (non-reasoning or reasoning-off model, cached prompt), TTS TTFB ≤150-250 ms measured to first audible byte, and ≤100-150 ms for network + telephony + jitter. That sums to ~0.9-1.3 s caller-side on PSTN and ~0.7-0.9 s on WebRTC; beating "vendor claims" of 400-600 ms is not required because no independent phone measurement shows any platform achieving them.
- The Cekura number for Pipecat (3.15 s median on defaults) versus Daily's own 508 ms P50 on one co-located GPU shows a ~6x spread for the same framework. The spread is mostly configuration (endpointing delays, hop count, telephony path), which is an argument that latency is a product/engineering differentiator rather than a framework property, and that an open stack's defaults matter commercially.
- Because platform-reported latency undercounts by ~0.5 s relative to the caller's ear, any internal SLO should be measured from recordings at the caller side (or with a synthetic caller), not from pipeline timestamps.
- P95 and "false cutoff rate" are better selection metrics than median latency: the evidence shows faster endpointing trades directly against interrupting callers mid-sentence (0 of 65+ stacks handled a 680 ms pause), so the latency budget and turn-accuracy budget must be set jointly.

### Gaps
- No Hamming AI, Artificial Analysis (voice-agent-level), Cresta or Coval primary publications with platform-level voice-to-voice percentiles were retrieved directly; Coval and Artificial Analysis figures are quoted via third parties. "Tested Media" (March 2026) could not be located as a primary source - it is known only through Telnyx, Eightpine and Orbit pages and should be treated cautiously.
- No controlled user study linking specific voice-agent response delays (e.g. 800 vs 1,200 ms) to abandonment or CSAT was found; the 800 ms and 1,500 ms thresholds are consensus heuristics. ITU-T G.114 one-way-delay guidance was not retrieved.
- No independent caller-side measurements of self-hosted open-source stacks over PSTN at scale (only vendor demos and the Cekura defaults run).
- Jitter-buffer/playout contributions are rarely itemized in published budgets.

## 3. Reliability, quality and compliance requirements in production

### Takeaway
Production requirements cluster into turn-taking quality (barge-in, backchannels, noise, false cutoffs), verifiable task outcomes with regression evals, multi-provider resilience, and a compliance chain (BAA down the subprocessor stack, consent, recording notice, numbering rules) that hosted platforms partially gate behind enterprise tiers. India's telephony rules changed materially in Sept-Nov 2026 (A2P pre-declaration on DLT, number-series mandates), and US outbound AI calls need prior express consent under the FCC's 2024 TCPA ruling.

### Cited Findings

**Turn-taking, interruptions, noise, accents**
- Interruption handling is three separate problems - barge-in, backchannels ("uh-huh"), and background noise that sounds like speech - each needing its own fix; after barge-in the app must trim the LLM history to what the caller actually heard — [V] [Twilio, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/insights/ai-voice-agent-interruption-handling)
- No built-in detector in 65+ tested stacks survived a 680 ms mid-sentence pause; recommendation is an application-level "completeness gate" (hold the turn when required slots are missing) — [I] [Codebridge](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/voice-ai-phone-agents-latency-benchmark)
- A very fast small LLM (Qwen3-4B, 45 ms first token) returned confirmation codes with the leading letter missing 7/7 times; exact-code accuracy tracked the LLM not the pipeline (GPT-4.1 mini 7/7 correct) — [I] [Codebridge](https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/voice-ai-phone-agents-latency-benchmark)
- Vapi showed false starts on 44 of 89 goodbye turns and 50 of 432 discarded turns in Openbenchmarks; in Cekura v1, 41 of 246 Vapi calls never connected; Retell passed 75.61% of scenarios on all three runs (best of eight) and scored 5/5 on interruption handling — [I, via A] [Telnyx compilation](https://telnyx.com/resources/voice-ai-agents-compared-latency)
- Noise handling is now a paid layer: LiveKit offers Krisp VIVA / ai-coustics voice isolation at $0.0012/min beyond allowance; Pipecat Cloud includes Krisp VIVA free to 10k min then $0.0015/min; voice isolation suits single speakers, background-noise suppression suits multi-speaker — [V] [LiveKit pricing](https://livekit.com/pricing); [V] [LiveKit noise docs](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/media/noise-cancellation); [V] [Pipecat Cloud pricing](https://www.daily.co/pricing/pipecat-cloud/)
- Phone audio arrives at 8 kHz while browser tests run at 16 kHz, so STT behaves differently on the line; test from real carriers and mobile devices — [A] [Dograh](https://www.dograh.com/hub/blogs/voice-ai-own-gpus); [A] [Scallar QA checklist, 17 Aug 2026](https://scallar.in/blog/ai-voice-agent-testing-qa-monitoring-checklist)
- India-specific quality gaps: inconsistencies across regional accents, code-switching and real-world conditions remain key challenges; only 13% of surveyed organisations have scaled voice AI across multiple journeys (31% limited production); over one-third support ≥3 Indian languages — [A] [Nasscom India Voice AI Readiness 2026](https://www.nasscom.in/knowledge-center/publications/india-voice-ai-readiness-2026?page=2)
- Suggested production KPIs: containment >70%, first-call resolution 70-85%, WER <8% English / <10% with noise; "LLM updates frequently break working voice agents, which is why dedicated regression tooling (Hamming, Cekura, Coval) is now standard" — [A] [Softcery](https://softcery.com/lab/custom-ai-voice-agents-the-ultimate-guide)
- OpenAI's guidance: measure task/tool outcomes, conversational timing, speech/language (accents, noise, language switches, names, numbers) and session reliability independently — [V] [OpenAI voice agents guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-agents)
- IVR navigation, long holds (15-30 min insurance calls), transfers and DTMF are first-class problems; Pipecat's WebSocket telephony integrations (Twilio/Telnyx/Plivo/Exotel) lack advanced call-centre features such as transfers, which need the SIP/PSTN route — [P] [xAGI Labs](https://xagi.in/blog/from-vapi-to-livekit-hard-won-lessons-building-production-voice-ai-agents); [A] [Dograh on Pipecat Cloud](https://www.dograh.com/hub/blogs/pipecat-cloud-pricing)

**Fallbacks, limits, monitoring**
- Example of provider failover as a design target: Cartesia Sonic primary with ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 fallback adding ~50 ms — [V] [Orbit by Devotel](https://orbit.devotel.io/en/benchmarks/latency)
- Concurrency caps are a reliability constraint on APIs: ElevenLabs TTS 2-15 concurrent requests by plan (excess queue), Cartesia 2-15, Groq developer tier 30 RPM on gpt-oss-120b vs Cerebras 1,000 RPM; Vapi 4/10/30 calls; Bland 10/50 — [A] [AssemblyAI TTS guide](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/tts-api-pricing); [A] [deAI, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.deai.org/news/cerebras-vs-groq); [V] [Vapi pricing](https://vapi.ai/pricing)
- Prepaid-credit exhaustion took a Vapi-based demo fully offline ("stopped at a threshold instead of degrading") - a who-holds-the-off-switch risk — [P] [GitHub discussion](https://github.com/baptistecristo/AI-agents-for-dumbphones/discussions/9)
- SLAs: Bland 99.9% uptime on all plans; Vapi 99% on Pro; Sarvam 99.9% claimed — [V] [Bland](https://www.bland.ai/blog/bland-ai-vs-retell-vs-vapi-vs-air); [V] [Vapi pricing](https://vapi.ai/pricing); [A] [Gnani](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/top-voice-ai-platforms-for-enterprise)

**Compliance**

- GDPR/residency: Retell states "we do not currently operate services within the European Union"; Vapi is US-hosted; neither Groq nor Cerebras documents a European endpoint; many EU public/regulated buyers require EU hosting as a procurement condition — [A] [CofounderBase](https://cofounderbase.com/tools/voice-ai-agent-platforms/); [P] [GitHub discussion](https://github.com/baptistecristo/AI-agents-for-dumbphones/discussions/9); [A] [Micdrop](https://micdrop.dev/blog/top-llms-voice-agents); [A] [Voice Agent Bible](https://voiceagentbible.com/compliance)
- US TCPA: FCC Declaratory Ruling 24-17 (8 Feb 2024) confirms AI-generated voices are "artificial or prerecorded voice"; outbound calls need prior express consent (written for marketing to mobiles) and must identify the responsible entity — [V, regulator] [FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)
- A consent-revocation order (FCC 26-67, released 1 Oct 2026, effective 25 Nov 2026) is reported by one compliance tracker; not verified against the FCC text — [A] [Voice Agent Bible, verified 27 Oct 2026](https://voiceagentbible.com/compliance)
- Recording consent: US federal law (18 U.S.C. 2511(2)(d)) is one-party; some states require all-party consent - follow the strictest applicable rule and disclose both recording and AI — [A] [voiceagents.fyi checklist](https://voiceagents.fyi/guides/ai-voice-agent-security-and-compliance-checklist.pdf)
- India TRAI (TCCCPR): senders register on DLT; promotional calls on the 140 series, service/transactional on 1600 (lenders' deadlines 1 Jan-1 Mar 2026), 1601 opened 10 Aug 2026 for utilities/courier/logistics; the Third Amendment (notified 18 Sep 2026) defines A2P calls to include autodialled, robocall and artificial-voice calls, requires pre-declaring A2P use and CLI ranges (undeclared = UCC), staged at 30/60/90 days (~18 Oct, ~17 Nov, ~17 Dec 2026) — [A] [Uservox, 4 Nov 2026](https://uservox.ai/blog/trai-rules-business-calls-2026/); [A] [Ozonetel, 28 Sep 2026](https://ozonetel.com/trai-ai-voice-call-rules/); [A] [Voice Agent Bible](https://voiceagentbible.com/compliance)
- The TRAI amendment text contains **no** duty to tell the called party the voice is AI; earlier reports of a disclosure duty were corrected; AI/ML "UCC_Detect" systems flag suspected spam CLIs within two hours and three complaints can trigger action — [A] [Voice Agent Bible](https://voiceagentbible.com/compliance); [A] [Botsense, 3 Sep 2026](https://botsense.io/blog/trai-ai-calling-rules-ucc-detection-signals); [A] [Ozonetel](https://ozonetel.com/trai-ai-voice-call-rules/)
- India calling hours/DPDP: RBI sales calls 09:00-19:00 and recovery 08:00-19:00 from 1 Jan 2027; DPDP treats voice recordings as personal data needing notice and lawful purpose; DPDP Rules consent-manager provisions 13 Nov 2026, substantive obligations 13 May 2027; RBI requires payment data stored in India and banks commonly demand in-country or on-prem deployment — [A] [Uservox](https://uservox.ai/blog/trai-rules-business-calls-2026/); [A] [Voice Agent Bible](https://voiceagentbible.com/compliance)
- India data residency among vendors: Bolna stores data in the US by default (India residency is enterprise); Sarvam offers cloud, VPC, on-prem and air-gapped; Ringg and Smallest.ai offer on-prem at enterprise tier — [A] [Kallix](https://www.kallix.in/ai-voice-agent-companies-india); [A] [Gnani](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/top-voice-ai-platforms-for-enterprise)

### Inferences
- The compliance chain is where self-hosting changes the risk profile most: every hosted hop is a subprocessor needing a BAA/DPA, whereas an open-source stack with self-hosted models reduces the chain to telephony + cloud. That is a concrete, sellable property in healthcare, BFSI and EU/India public sector, independent of per-minute cost.
- For India outbound, compliance (DLT registration, number series, A2P pre-declaration, DND scrubbing, RBI hours) is a product feature a platform must ship; a stack that ignores it will have its CLIs flagged by operator AI within hours. The second-stage A2P declaration requirement takes effect around the date of this note (~17 Nov 2026).
- Reliability evidence favours treating turn-detection accuracy, exact-entity accuracy (codes, numbers, names) and call-connect rate as first-class SLOs alongside latency; small fast models buy latency at a measurable accuracy cost.

### Gaps
- No published production failure-rate datasets (e.g. Hamming's call-analysis statistics) were retrieved; reliability evidence is from benchmarks and anecdotes.
- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations for voice agents and their effective dates were not retrieved from a primary source.
- A state-by-state list of US all-party recording consent states was not retrieved from a primary legal source.
- TRAI/FCC 2026 details are from secondary compliance trackers and vendor blogs; the gazette text and FCC 26-67 were not read directly. Hallucination/guardrail incident data specific to voice agents was not found.

## 4. Build-vs-buy lessons: migrations between hosted platforms and Pipecat/LiveKit stacks

### Takeaway
Published first-person migrations in 2026 run one way - Vapi/Retell → LiveKit or Pipecat - and report per-minute cost falling by ~50-75% (e.g. $0.26→$0.13, $0.10→$0.025, $0.09→$0.035), with the stated motives being debuggability and control of turn-taking as much as cost. The same authors consistently advise staying on a managed platform before product-market fit or below roughly 20k min/month, and warn that telephony and turn-taking are what hurt.

- Structural change affecting build-vs-buy on Pipecat specifically: on 29 Oct 2026 Deepgram announced it is acquiring Daily (Pipecat's maintainer); Pipecat stays BSD-2 and moves to an independent Pipecat Foundation; Daily's team joins Deepgram, whose new Voice Agent Platform will be built on Pipecat and Daily's network — [V] [Deepgram announcement](https://deepgram.com/blog/daily-pipecat-deepgram)

### Inferences
- Consistent decision rule across independent practitioners: start managed; migrate when (a) volume passes ~20k-100k min/month, (b) conversation quality is the product and you need to tune turn-taking, or (c) compliance/residency forces ownership. Cost is rarely the first trigger; an undiagnosable incident usually is.
- The practical migration enabler is an internal abstraction (call identity, lifecycle events, configuration contract) built while still on the managed platform; this is also a product opportunity (a Vapi/Retell-compatible config or import path on top of an open stack).
- Reported savings (50-75%) are infra-only and mix provider swaps with platform-fee removal; none of the write-ups publish fully loaded engineering cost, so they overstate net savings at low volume.
- The Deepgram-Daily deal lowers perceived bus-factor risk for Pipecat (foundation governance) but makes its primary commercial steward a speech-model vendor competing with other STT/TTS providers; vendor-neutrality in practice is something to watch.

### Gaps
- I found no published case of a team moving the **reverse** direction (self-hosted Pipecat/LiveKit → Vapi/Retell/ElevenLabs) with reasons and numbers; survivorship bias in the migration literature is likely.
- No detailed post-mortem of a failed self-hosting attempt (outage, cost overrun) was found.
- No Hacker News or Reddit threads with verifiable figures were retrieved; practitioner evidence is from personal/company blogs, several by consultants who sell migrations.
- Almost all first-person migrations target LiveKit; quantified Pipecat production migrations at scale were not found.

### Inferences
- A generic "cheaper Vapi on open source" is the weakest position: hosting is already $0.01/min from the framework owners, bundled agent APIs are at $0.03-$0.075/min, and investors explicitly call the orchestration/model layer commoditized. Cost leadership alone only differentiates at high volume or in markets with a lower price floor (India at ₹3-6/min).
- The strongest evidence-backed gaps for a new product on an open stack are: (1) vertical "close the call" products with deep system-of-record integration; (2) deployable-anywhere packaging (VPC/on-prem/air-gapped with a short subprocessor chain) for BFSI, healthcare, government and EU/India residency, a segment that is ~27% of revenue and that hosted leaders gate behind enterprise contracts; (3) Indic and other low-resource language quality beyond Hindi (code-switching, the 11-vs-22 language TTS gap) plus built-in TRAI/DLT compliance; (4) evals, simulation and caller-side latency/turn-accuracy observability, the fastest-growing component; (5) voice-specific inference (warm, co-located, high-concurrency STT/LLM/TTS serving), which Bessemer names as open and for which no credible public concurrency benchmarks exist.
- Turn-taking quality is a differentiator that is both measurable (false-cutoff rate at a latency budget) and currently unsolved in every tested product; a product that publishes caller-side P50/P95 and false-cutoff numbers would stand out against vendor claims that independent tests show are 2-20x optimistic.
- Cascaded remains the enterprise default through 2026 for control and compliance, so an open cascaded stack is not on the wrong side of the architecture shift yet, but a hybrid router (cascaded by default, speech-to-speech for selected calls) is the hedge the eval vendors recommend.
- Market-size reports disagree by ~2x on the 2025 base ($2.5B-$4.9B) and on CAGR (22%-39%); they are paywalled syndicated research with unpublished methods and should be cited as a range, with the Menlo spend figure and Twilio's reported voice growth as the more concrete demand signals.
