Lesson 8 · Part II — The voice loop

Speech to Text

⏱ 40 minLevel: intermediate

STT is the bot's ears: audio frames in, text frames out. Every provider is a drop-in STTService — learn the pattern once.

Audio in, text out

Two kinds of STT service

BaseHow it worksLatency
STTService (streaming)Persistent WebSocket; audio streams continuously.Lowest.
SegmentedSTTService (HTTP)Local VAD chunks speech; each segment POSTed as a WAV.Higher (per-segment).

They're interchangeable in the pipeline — swap providers with no other change. The one method a provider overrides is run_stt(audio) -> AsyncGenerator.

Configuring STT

from pipecat.services.deepgram.stt import DeepgramSTTService
from pipecat.transcriptions.language import Language

stt = DeepgramSTTService(
    api_key=os.environ["DEEPGRAM_API_KEY"],
    settings=DeepgramSTTService.Settings(
        language=Language.EN,        # or "multi" for multilingual (Deepgram); None = auto (Whisper)
    ),
)

🐍 Base-class knobs worth knowing

audio_passthrough=True (default) lets audio continue downstream so things like recording still work. Set sample rates once in PipelineParams (audio_in_sample_rate), not per service.

STT latency tuning

Each STT service has a measured P99 latency for delivering a final transcript after speech stops. The turn logic uses this to decide how long to wait before ending the user's turn. If the bot responds too early or too late, tune this — it's broadcast as an STTMetadataFrame and consumed by the stop strategy (Lesson 10).

✅ Checkpoint

1. What does an STTService output, and what feeds it?

Text frames (TranscriptionFrame/InterimTranscriptionFrame); it's fed audio frames from the transport (after VAD).

2. Streaming vs segmented — which is lower latency and why?

Streaming (STTService) — a persistent connection sends audio continuously, versus batching WAV segments over HTTP.
